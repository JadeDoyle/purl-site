Draw a chart

By the end of this you will have drawn a colourwork chart, seen it as knitted fabric, saved it as a PDF, and turned a photo into a second chart.

Part one: draw a chart

  1. Tap More, then Chart maker.
  2. Tap New chart.
  3. Type the size. One square is one stitch, so try 20 for both Stitches (width) and Rows (height).
  4. Tap Create chart.
  1. Tap a colour in the strip along the bottom to select it.
  2. Draw on the grid with one finger. Pencil is already chosen.
  3. To pan, start the drag outside the grid. To zoom, pinch. To abandon a stroke halfway, touch down a second finger.
  1. Tap + at the end of the colour strip to add a second colour, then Use this colour.
  2. Tap Select, drag a box around your motif, then tap Repeat to tile it across and down. One tap on Undo takes the whole repeat back if you do not like it.
  1. Tap Preview under the grid.
  2. Type your Gauge per 10 cm into Sts and Rows. The line at the bottom now says what the piece measures.
  3. Try the 3D, 2D and Chart looks, and step Repeat the chart up to see the motif tiled.
  1. Go back, then tap the share icon in the header and choose PDF. That is the printable version, numbered on all four edges.

Part two: turn a photo into a chart

  1. Go back to Chart maker and tap From a photo.
  2. Tap Take a photo or Choose from library.
  3. Drag the corners of the box to frame the part you want.
  1. Set Stitches wide. The line underneath tells you how many rows that works out as.
  2. Set Colours. Start low, around four to six, and add more only if the picture needs them.
  3. Turn on Correct for knitting gauge and type your Sts / 10 cm and Rows / 10 cm. Stitches are wider than they are tall, so this adds rows to keep the picture's shape when it is knitted.
  4. Tap Chart at the top to see the result, and Photo to compare.
  5. Tap Create chart. The chart is saved and opens in the editor, where you can tidy it up by hand.

If it goes wrong

  • You painted a stroke you did not mean to. Touch a second finger down before you lift, and the stroke is dropped. If you already lifted, tap Undo.
  • The grid will not move. Panning has to start outside the grid. A drag that begins on a square paints instead.
  • The photo chart looks squashed on screen. That is Correct for knitting gauge doing its job. The chart looks tall because the knitted result will not be.
