User guide

How to use Purl. Plain language, no code, no assumptions about programming or technical background.

This is the written companion to the in-app help (empty states, first-run tour, tooltips). Read here if you want to sit down and learn; everything inside the app gives you the same answers in the moment you need them.

Note: if you find anything that seems off, or if you have any questions, please send us feedback through the app, or by e-mail contact@purl.no.

Tutorials

Short, one sitting each. Follow them in order if Purl is new to you.

  • Your first project: open a pattern, let Purl make the project for you, then put your own yarn in it.
  • Read a pattern: keep your place in a PDF row by row, follow a chart inside it, and keep the key on screen while you work.
  • Get your yarn in: scan a ball band instead of typing it, weigh yarn down as you use it, and turn what you are short of into a shopping list.
  • Make a chart: draw colourwork square by square, see it as knitted fabric, and turn a photo into a chart.

Reference

Look things up here once you know your way around.

  • Yarn stash: everything you own, with colours, dye lots, fibre and how much is left.
  • Patterns: getting patterns in, writing your own, and sharing them.
  • Projects: the thing you are making, and how pattern, yarn, progress and photos hang off it.
  • PDF tools: the eight tools that sit on top of a PDF pattern while you knit.
  • Colour charts: drawing charts, seeing them as knitted fabric, and exporting them.
  • Crochet charts: drawing crochet symbol charts by hand or from written rows and rounds, and exporting them.
  • Barcodes and the yarn library: why a scanned ball band fills itself in, and how to build your own library.
  • Backups and recovery: getting something back after you delete it, and moving everything to a new device.
  • Terminology glossary: Norwegian and English knitting and crochet abbreviations, side by side.
  • Calculator: gauge, resizing, even shaping and how many skeins to buy.
  • FAQ: known limitations, common questions, things that look like bugs but are not.
